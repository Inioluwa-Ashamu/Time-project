Sensory perception and autism

Usually when we look at sensory issues in autism, we think about things like someone being over or under-sensitive to stimuli, or getting overwhelmed by too much information. Here, we look at how our brains make sense of information received by our sense organs so that we can understand it and decide how to respond. This is known as perception.

We often hear autism described as brains that are wired differently, so we can assume that sensory perception might happen differently in autistic people compared to the predominant neurotype.

Understanding a bit more about perception can give us better insight into how autistic people may experience things differently, which can help us support them more effectively.

1. Watch the following video about the way our brains use sensory information to make sense of the world.

Diabetes Awareness Training

This Diabetes Awareness Training was produced by Ally, a member of the Time Specialist Support office team.

The training raises awareness around diabetes. It was produced because one of our clients is diabetic and at least one member of staff is required to have awareness on his sessions. It is also useful for all support staff to understand more about what life may be like for someone with diabetes and how best to offer support if needed.
