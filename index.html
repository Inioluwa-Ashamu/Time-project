People first

Support begins with the right relationship.

Families are supported by a small office team who coordinate carefully, listen closely, and help match each young person with workers who fit their interests, pace, and needs.

What matters in practice

Autism-specific support Enhanced DBS checked staff Safeguarding-aware practice Clear family communication Community-based confidence building

How it works

A straightforward route from first conversation to ongoing support.

1

Tell us what support would help

We listen to the family context, the young person's interests, communication style, routines, sensory needs, and any practical pressures around timing or location.

2

We look for the right match

Matching considers personality, interests, availability, experience, and the kind of relationship likely to help trust grow steadily.

3

Support starts with preparation

We plan the first sessions carefully, keep communication clear, and review what is working as the relationship develops.

Why families choose Time

Good support is often about the small things being done consistently well.

Parents need to know that support is safe, prepared, and genuinely interested in their child. We pay attention to the everyday details that make families feel less alone with the planning.

Thoughtful matching

We look at interests, routine, personality, confidence, and practical fit.

Prepared support workers

Staff are enhanced DBS checked, trained, and properly prepared before working alone.

Clear communication

Families and professionals know who to contact, what is happening, and what to expect next.

Trust and reassurance

Parents are not just buying hours of support. They are trusting someone with their child.

That is why our service is built around safer recruitment, thoughtful matching, autism-specific preparation, and clear communication with families.

Safer recruitment Enhanced DBS checks, references, induction, and ongoing support for workers.
Autism-specific practice Support planned around communication, sensory needs, routines, and interests.
Local understanding Community support across Manchester, Salford, Stockport, and nearby areas.
Open communication Families have clear routes for questions, feedback, concerns, and next steps.

In practice

Sessions are planned around real people, not a fixed script.

A good session might be quiet and familiar, active and confidence-building, or focused on helping a young person try something new at their own pace, from a local walk to a specialist activity.

Before Understand interests, routines, communication, sensory needs, and family priorities.
During Support community access, respite care, shared activities, confidence, independence, and enjoyment.
After Keep families informed and notice what is working, changing, or worth trying next.
