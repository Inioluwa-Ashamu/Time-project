About Time Specialist Support

Kind, well-organised support for families who need consistency.

Good support is never just about filling hours. It is about building the right relationship, communicating clearly, and making everyday life feel a little more manageable for the young person and the people around them.

Thoughtful matching

We look at personality, interests, routine, practical needs, and availability to build support that feels natural rather than forced.

Confidence through consistency

Families need reliable people and reliable communication. We focus on both, so support is stable and easier to trust.

Better access to community life

From routine outings to specialist activities, support is designed to help young people participate in ways that feel safe and achievable.

What we do

How support usually begins and grows.

01

Understand the situation

We start by listening carefully to the young person, family, and wider context so support is rooted in real needs rather than assumptions.

02

Match the right worker

Compatibility matters. We consider personality, interests, confidence, and practical requirements before a placement begins.

03

Prepare properly

Workers are supported with autism-specific training, safeguarding expectations, and trial sessions alongside experienced colleagues.

04

Review and adapt

Good support evolves. We stay responsive to feedback, changes in confidence, and changes in family circumstances.

For families and referrers

Clear from the first conversation.

Families and professionals need to know who they are speaking to, how the service works, and what good support will look like in practice. We keep that side of things simple, clear, and easy to follow.

Work for us

Support work that is meaningful, flexible, and rooted in relationships.

We are always interested in people who are calm, reliable, and genuinely motivated to support autistic young people well. Experience helps, but warmth, patience, and good judgment matter just as much.

The role suits people who value consistency, notice the small details, and enjoy helping young people feel more comfortable and confident.

Many support workers are balancing study or building experience, but the work is never treated casually. Families rely on us to get the basics right every time.

What matters most is being dependable, respectful, and ready to build trust over time.
