How it works

From first conversation to support that feels settled.

1. Tell us about the young person

Start with a conversation about the young person, family routines, interests, communication, sensory needs, and what would make life feel steadier.

2. We match carefully

We think about fit, personality, interests, location, availability, and readiness before introducing a support worker.

3. Support starts and keeps being reviewed

Sessions are reviewed as confidence grows, so support can remain practical, enjoyable, and useful for the whole family.

Why families choose Time

Trust is built into the practical details.

Turning up when expected. Matching people carefully. Explaining things clearly. Not rushing trust. Thinking ahead. These details shape how the service feels in real life.

  • Support workers are recruited carefully and DBS checked where required for the role.
  • New workers are prepared before supporting independently in the community.
  • The office team coordinates matching, communication, and day-to-day service quality.
  • Families have clear routes for questions, feedback, concerns, and complaints.

Accountable communication

In practice

Sessions are planned around real people, not a fixed script.

A good session might be quiet and familiar, active and confidence-building, or focused on helping a young person try something new at their own pace.

Before Understand interests, routines, communication, sensory needs, and family priorities.
During Support community access, shared activities, confidence, independence, and enjoyment.
After Keep families informed and notice what is working, changing, or worth trying next.
