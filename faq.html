FAQs

Questions families often ask.

A calm starting point for understanding how matching works, what support can look like, and what to include when you first contact us.

Starting support

You do not need every answer before you get in touch.

If you are not sure what support should look like yet, that is completely normal. A first conversation can help make the next step clearer.

What support do you provide?

Autism-specific support for children, young people, and some adults, often around community access, activities, confidence, routine, and family breathing space.

Who is the service for?

Families looking for thoughtful, relationship-led support for an autistic child or young person, and professionals wanting to discuss whether Time is a suitable option.

How do we start?

Contact the office with a short outline of the support needed, the area you live in, and the best way to reach you. The team will guide the next step.

Do you accept professional enquiries?

Yes. Professionals can use the contact form or email the office to discuss support, referrals, and practical questions before families take the next step.

Matching and sessions

Support works best when it is prepared, personal, and paced well.

How do you choose a support worker?

Matching considers interests, communication style, personality, routine, location, availability, confidence, and practical fit.

What if my child needs time to build trust?

Support can begin gently, with familiar routines, preparation, and a pace that respects the young person's confidence.

Can support happen outside the home?

Yes. Many sessions involve community access, specialist activities, shared interests, independence, confidence building, and enjoyable experiences.

Will we know what is happening?

Families should have clear contact routes, practical updates, and a team who can talk through changes, questions, and concerns.

Safety and expectations

Safeguarding and reliability are part of the service.

Support workers are recruited through safer recruitment processes, including enhanced DBS checks where required for the role, training, and preparation before working alone.

Families can raise questions, concerns, compliments, or complaints through the office, and the contact page explains how those messages are routed.

The contact form is not monitored for emergencies. If someone is in immediate danger, call 999 or contact the relevant safeguarding service.

Use the guided prompts on the contact page. They will help you start a message without needing to write everything from scratch.
