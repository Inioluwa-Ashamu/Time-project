What we do

Autism-specific support designed for confidence, respite, access, and continuity.

We support autistic children and young people through carefully matched, practical support that helps them access community activities, specialist activities, and new experiences with confidence.

Book a free consultationRead parent FAQs

Befriending and community support

Support workers accompany a young person to community activities, outings, and specialist activities in ways that build familiarity, enjoyment, and confidence.

Specialist activity access

We support attendance at specialist activities with preparation that reflects interests, communication needs, sensory needs, and comfort levels.

Respite and family communication

Families receive agreed touchpoints and expectations, so support creates useful time at home rather than another layer of planning stress.

How support takes shape

Not a fixed package. A service that adjusts around the person.

A

Interest-led matching

Matches are informed by interests, availability, likely compatibility, location, and preferences where relevant.

B

Visual preparation

Social stories and visual resources can be used to explain upcoming support or community activities.

C

Prepared staff

Workers are enhanced DBS checked and prepared with autism-specific training and practical strategies.

D

Shadowing and readiness

New team members complete trial sessions with more experienced staff before working independently.

How it works

What happens before support begins.

1

Initial conversation

We ask about the young person, family priorities, location, funding route, and the type of support that would help.

2

Matching and preparation

We consider interests, personality, communication, sensory needs, availability, and preferences that matter.

3

Start, review, adapt

Support starts carefully, with family communication and space to adjust as confidence and routines develop.

Typical focus areas

Support can include:

Community confidence

Helping young people feel more comfortable accessing places, activities, and routines outside the home.

Interest-led engagement

Using existing interests to build connection, predictability, and positive shared experiences.

Communication support

Using clear language, planning, and visual resources where helpful.

Family respite

Providing support that is reliable enough to create rest, structure, and confidence at home.
