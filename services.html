What we do

Autism-specific support designed for confidence, access, and continuity.

We support autistic children and young people through carefully matched, practical support that helps them access community activities, specialist activities, and new experiences with confidence.

Befriending and community support

Support workers accompany a young person to community activities, outings, and specialist activities in ways that build familiarity, enjoyment, and confidence.

Specialist activity access

We support attendance at specialist activities with preparation that reflects the young person's interests, communication needs, and comfort levels.

Family communication

Families receive clearer touchpoints and expectations, so practical details do not become another layer of stress.

How support takes shape

Not a fixed package. A service that adjusts around the person.

Some young people need confidence and familiarity in the community. Others need more preparation, a gentler pace, or careful planning around sensory needs, routine, and how new activities are introduced.

Our job is to make support feel considered rather than generic, while keeping families and professionals clear about what is happening and why.

What makes the service different

Preparation is part of the service, not an afterthought.

A

Interest-led matching

Matches are informed by interests, availability, likely compatibility, and gender preferences where relevant.

B

Visual preparation

Social stories and visual resources can be used to explain upcoming support or community activities in a more accessible way.

C

Autism-informed staffing

Workers are enhanced DBS checked and prepared with autism-specific training and practical strategies before they start supporting families.

D

Shadowing and readiness

New team members complete at least two trial sessions with more experienced staff before working independently in the community.

Who we support

Support for young people, with the whole family in mind.

A good service has to work beyond the session itself. It should ease the planning burden, reduce uncertainty, and create support that families can actually rely on.

Young people

Encouraging participation, confidence, and safe access to meaningful experiences.

Parents and carers

Reliable communication, clearer expectations, and more breathing room day to day.

Professionals

A better explained service model that is easier to discuss, refer to, and trust.

Typical focus areas

Support can include:

Building confidence in the community

Helping young people feel more comfortable accessing places, activities, and routines outside the home.

Encouraging engagement around interests

Using existing interests as a way to build connection, predictability, and positive shared experiences.

Supporting smoother communication

Using clear language, planning, and visual resources where helpful to make support easier to understand.

Reducing some of the pressure on families

Providing support that is reliable enough to create breathing room and a stronger sense of structure.
