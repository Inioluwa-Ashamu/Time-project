What we do

Autism-specific support for confidence, community access, and continuity.

We carefully match autistic children and young people with workers who can help them access activities, build confidence, and try new experiences at a pace that feels right.

Befriending and community support

Support workers accompany a young person to community activities, outings, and specialist activities in ways that build familiarity, enjoyment, and confidence.

Activity access

We support attendance at specialist activities with preparation that reflects the young person's interests, communication needs, and comfort levels.

Family communication

Families receive clearer touchpoints and expectations, so practical details do not become another layer of stress.

How support takes shape

Planned around the person, not a fixed package.

Some young people need confidence and familiarity in the community. Others need more preparation, a gentler pace, or careful planning around sensory needs, routine, and how new activities are introduced.

Our job is to make each session feel considered, while keeping families and professionals clear about what is happening and why.

Talk to us about support

Why preparation matters

Preparation helps support feel safer and more settled.

A

Interest-led matching

Matches are informed by interests, availability, likely compatibility, and gender preferences where relevant.

B

Visual preparation

Social stories and visual resources can be used to explain upcoming support or community activities in a more accessible way.

C

Autism-informed staffing

Workers are enhanced DBS checked and prepared with autism-specific training and practical strategies before they start supporting families.

D

Shadowing and readiness

New team members complete at least two trial sessions with more experienced staff before working independently in the community.

What families can expect

Clear support from first enquiry onwards.

Trust grows when families know what is happening, who is involved, and how to ask questions if something needs more thought.

  • A named office team coordinating enquiries, matching, and practical communication.
  • Support planned around interests, communication, sensory needs, routine, and family priorities.
  • Preparation before sessions, with review as confidence, needs, or circumstances change.
  • A clear contact route for questions, compliments, concerns, and complaints.

Who we support

Support for young people, with the whole family in mind.

Good support has to work beyond the session itself. It should reduce uncertainty, ease planning, and give families something they can rely on.

Young people

Encouraging participation, confidence, and safe access to meaningful experiences.

Parents and carers

Reliable communication, clearer expectations, and more breathing room day to day.

Professionals

A straightforward service to discuss, refer to, and trust.

Typical focus areas

Support can include:

Building confidence in the community

Helping young people feel more comfortable accessing places, activities, and routines outside the home.

Encouraging engagement around interests

Using existing interests as a way to build connection, predictability, and positive shared experiences.

Supporting smoother communication

Using clear language, planning, and visual resources where helpful to make support easier to understand.

Reducing some of the pressure on families

Creating reliable sessions that give families breathing room and a stronger sense of structure.

Common questions

Questions parents and carers often ask.

You do not need to have every detail worked out before contacting us. These are the kinds of things we can talk through together.

What if my child needs time to build trust?

Support can start gently, with preparation, familiar routines, and a pace that respects the young person’s confidence.

How do you choose a support worker?

We consider interests, communication style, location, availability, experience, and practical fit before suggesting a match.

Can support happen outside the home?

Yes. Many sessions focus on community access, activities, confidence, independence, and enjoyable shared experiences.

Will we know what is happening?

Families should have clear contact routes, practical updates, and a team who can talk through changes or concerns.
