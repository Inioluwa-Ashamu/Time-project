About us

A service built on trust, communication, and autism-specific understanding.

Time Specialist Support carefully matches autistic children, young people, and some adults with workers who are prepared, respectful, and genuinely interested in who they are.

Our purpose

The right support starts with listening.

Every child, young person, and family is different. Routines, sensory needs, confidence levels, and practical pressures all shape what helpful support looks like.

When families get in touch, we take time to understand the person, the family context, and what would make day-to-day life feel steadier.

A note from Tori

Support works best when it feels personal, prepared, and steady.

Founder and Director

Time Specialist Support grew from a belief that autistic children and young people deserve support that is thoughtful, well matched, and genuinely interested in who they are.

Families often come to us at points where day-to-day life feels stretched. In those moments, small things matter: feeling listened to, knowing who to contact, and having confidence that support has been matched with care.

We want the service to feel dependable from the outset, not just in principle but in the details of how we communicate, prepare, and follow through.

Person-led support

We adapt around communication style, sensory needs, interests, and routine rather than expecting a young person to fit a fixed way of working.

Families matter too

Good support should ease pressure for the whole family, whether that means a regular break, occasional weekend help, or extra support during the holidays.

Quality through preparation

Matching, training, DBS checks, and trial sessions all contribute to safer, more confident, and more sustainable support.

Standards

Standards families can feel in practice.

Trust is not only about warmth. It also depends on clear responsibilities, careful recruitment, preparation, and honest follow-up when something needs attention.

Matching well

Thinking carefully about fit, not just filling availability.

Communicating clearly

Keeping expectations, updates, and next steps easy to follow.

Safeguarding and preparation

Safer recruitment, DBS checks, training, and preparation help support feel more secure from the start.

Reviewing honestly

Being open about what is working, what is changing, and what needs more thought.

How we think

How we want support to feel.

Respect

We support people in ways that preserve dignity, voice, and individuality.

Clarity

Families and referrers should always understand what support looks like and why.

Consistency

Confidence grows when support is reliable, prepared, and professionally coordinated.

Warmth

Professionalism matters, but support should still feel human, approachable, and calm.
